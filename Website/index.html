The Problem
Three risks sitting in your firm right now

Most Australian law firms have AI running in the background. No documentation, no policy, and no clear picture of what's exposed.

🔓
Client data crossing borders

Under APP 8 of the Privacy Act 1988, firms must take reasonable steps before personal information is disclosed overseas. US-hosted AI tools like ChatGPT, Copilot and Grammarly may trigger this obligation every time a staff member uses them with client content.

📋
No documented compliance position

The Legal Profession Uniform Law requires firms to demonstrate competence in their use of technology. Without a written AI usage policy and staff training records, a firm has no evidence it has addressed the risk, even if it has.

💸
Subscriptions running without oversight

Staff add AI tools constantly. Zylo's research tracking 30 million licences found 53% go unused within 30 days. Most firms can't name every AI subscription currently running across their team. Until they look.

Security & Privacy
Built for firms that ask hard questions

We know your IT team or a managing partner will want to know exactly how this works. Here's the honest picture.

🗄️
Your data stays in your browser

All audit inputs, including firm name, tool list and compliance answers, are held in browser memory only. They are not stored on Theia-Stack servers. When you close the session, the data is gone.

🔐
API key never exposed

Claude API calls are routed through a Netlify serverless function. Your API key is never transmitted to the browser. All data is in transit over HTTPS/TLS 1.3.

⚖️
APP 8 transparency

Report generation sends your firm profile to the Anthropic API, which operates on US servers. Under APP 8, we recommend not including personal client information in audit inputs. No client names or matter details are required.

🔒
Zero data retention

Anthropic's commercial API operates under a zero-data-retention policy by default. Prompt data is not used to train models and is not retained after the API call completes.

🛡️
Notifiable Data Breaches

In the event of a platform security incident, affected firms will be notified within 72 hours in accordance with the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme under Part IIIC of the Privacy Act 1988.

📥
Your documents, always yours

Your generated policy and report can be downloaded and stored locally at any time. In the event of platform unavailability, your compliance documentation remains accessible offline.

ℹ️
Theia-Stack is built on Netlify (Sydney edge, global CDN) and Anthropic infrastructure. Both platforms hold SOC 2 Type II certification. For IT vendor assessments or additional security documentation, email hello@theiastack.com.au.

FAQ
Common questions.
What is the AI Risk Management Report?+
The AI Risk Management Report is a timestamped document generated by the Theia-Stack platform that records your firm's AI tool stack, compliance risk assessment, AI usage policy and staff training status. Every finding is mapped to a specific Australian legal obligation, including the Privacy Act 1988 Australian Privacy Principles and the Legal Profession Uniform Law. The report is dated on issue and expires after 12 months, at which point it must be renewed to keep your documentation current. It is self-issued by the platform. It is not externally accredited, and we won't claim otherwise. What it gives your firm is a documented, dated compliance position.
We're a small firm — is this relevant for us?+
Yes. The Privacy Act obligations around AI tool use apply regardless of firm size. APP 8 cross-border disclosure obligations, for example, are triggered the moment a staff member submits client content to an overseas AI service like ChatGPT or Grammarly. Thomson Reuters' 2024 Australian research found that 31% of law firm staff are already using unofficial AI tools without firm knowledge. Theia-Stack pricing is flat-rate per firm, not per user, which means smaller firms get the same value at the same price as larger ones.
What Australian compliance obligations are relevant?+
Four frameworks are directly relevant to Australian law firms using AI tools. The Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), specifically APP 3 (collection), APP 6 (use and disclosure), APP 8 (cross-border disclosure) and APP 11 (security), applies to all personal information processed by AI systems. The Legal Profession Uniform Law imposes a duty of competence in technology use and a duty of confidentiality that applies regardless of which tool is used. The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (Royal Assent December 2024) introduces new transparency obligations for automated decision-making. The OAIC's October 2024 AI guidance also requires firms to conduct due diligence on AI vendors before submitting personal information. The Theia-Stack report maps every finding to these specific obligations.
Do you need access to our systems or client files?+
No. The audit runs entirely in your browser. No system access, no integration, and no client files are required or requested at any point. The fastest way to identify your AI tool stack is to paste your firm's credit card or billing statement into the tool, which automatically reads and identifies subscriptions. If you'd prefer not to do that, there's a manual checklist option. The only information that leaves your browser is your firm profile: name, staff count, practice areas, tool names and compliance questionnaire answers, which is sent to the Anthropic API to generate your report. No client names, matter numbers or document content should ever be entered into the platform.
